The Quiet Cabin

    Why does the engine fade but not the voices?

    You are on a 6-hour flight. You switch on your noise-canceling headphones. The engine rumble gets much quieter, yet you can still hear the person next to you. In this lab you listen, read some sample data, sketch what you think is going on, and check some ideas. This replaces the paper Worksheet 1.

    Goal (same for everyone)Describe patterns in sound-level data and draw an initial model with questions that can be investigated.
    No wrong answers yetYour first model is a starting point. You will compare it with your final model near the end of the unit.
    About 25 minutesListen and data about 10 minutes, model about 10, ideas check and exit about 5.
    Keep the volume low or medium. Stop any sound that is uncomfortable. Every sound also has a picture and a description, so you can do the whole lab without listening.
    Choices you have: larger text (top right), a word bank in English and Spanish, drawing or photo or words for your model, and voice typing from your device's keyboard in any text box.
    For teachers

    Matches Worksheet 1 Parts A to C and the exit ticket, for Lesson 1 (Engage). The two sounds are synthesized in the page, not recordings, so the voices are babble without words; use real clips if you have them. The noise-canceling switch in step 2 plays the sample data (a 16 dB drop for the rumble, a 1 dB drop for the voices); it is a model of the data, not real headphones, and it does not explain how canceling works. Answers stay in the student's browser while they work. Nothing is sent to you: students save a file at the end and turn it in the way you choose. The Wave Ideas Check shows no answers; use the saved files to sort misconceptions.
